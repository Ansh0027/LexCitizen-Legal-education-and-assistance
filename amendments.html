The
amendments.

The Constitution changes through a deliberate process. These landmark amendments show how India has adapted its institutions, expanded participation, and tested the balance between Parliament, courts, and rights.

Landmark moments

How the framework evolved

08 amendments
01st Amendment · 1951

Land reform, speech, and the Ninth Schedule

The first amendment responded to land reform challenges and clarified the State's power to make certain special provisions.

What changed

  • Added Articles 15(4), 31A, 31B, and the Ninth Schedule.
  • Added specific grounds for reasonable restrictions on speech.
Why it matters: It began the long constitutional conversation about social reform, property, and judicial review.
07th Amendment · 1956

States reorganised

India's internal map was reorganised, replacing the earlier classification of states and supporting administration in a linguistic and federal country.

What changed

  • Reworked the constitutional structure of states and territories.
  • Created a more coherent framework for interstate administration.
Why it matters: Federalism is not static; constitutional geography affects representation, services, and identity.
42nd Amendment · 1976

The “Mini-Constitution”

During the Emergency, this amendment made wide-ranging changes to the Preamble, Parliament, courts, Directive Principles, and Fundamental Duties.

What changed

  • Added “Socialist,” “Secular,” and “Integrity” to the Preamble.
  • Added Fundamental Duties in Article 51A.
  • Expanded the emphasis on Directive Principles.
Later balance: The 44th Amendment later restored several safeguards and strengthened constitutional liberty.
44th Amendment · 1978

Restoring constitutional safeguards

After the Emergency, this amendment protected civil liberties and changed the constitutional treatment of property and emergencies.

What changed

  • Made right to property a legal right under Article 300A, not a Fundamental Right.
  • Strengthened safeguards around declaring and continuing an Emergency.
  • Protected Articles 20 and 21 from suspension during Emergency.
Why it matters: Amendments can repair institutional imbalance as well as create new policy.
73rd & 74th Amendments · 1992

Power closer to people

These amendments gave constitutional status to Panchayats and Municipalities and created a framework for local self-government.

What changed

  • Added Parts IX and IXA and the Eleventh and Twelfth Schedules.
  • Established elected local bodies, reservations, and regular elections.
Why it matters: Local government brings democratic decisions closer to neighbourhoods and villages.
86th Amendment · 2002

Education as a constitutional right

Article 21A made free and compulsory education a Fundamental Right for children between six and fourteen years.

What changed

  • Added Article 21A.
  • Connected the right to education with a duty of parents or guardians.
In practice: The Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education Act, 2009 gives the right a statutory framework.

Article 368

How an amendment happens

  • A bill may be introduced in either House of Parliament.
  • It generally needs a special majority.
  • Some federal changes also need ratification by at least half the states.
  • The President gives assent after Parliament passes it.

The basic structure

Power to amend is not unlimited.

The Supreme Court has held that Parliament cannot destroy the Constitution's basic structure, including principles such as judicial review, democracy, secularism, and federalism.
