Article 51A · Part IVA

Fundamental
duties.

The Constitution does not only describe what citizens may claim. It also sets out the civic responsibilities that help a democratic society remain free, fair, and livable.

Article 51A

What citizenship asks of us

11 duties
01 · Constitutional respect

Respect the Constitution

Abide by the Constitution, respect its ideals and institutions, the National Flag, and the National Anthem.

In practice

  • Understand the constitutional values behind public institutions.
  • Respect national symbols without silencing lawful criticism.
Important: A duty is a constitutional principle; it does not automatically create a criminal offence.
02 · Freedom's history

Remember the ideals of the freedom struggle

Treasure and follow the noble ideals that inspired India's struggle for independence.

In practice

  • Value liberty, equality, pluralism, and self-government.
  • Learn how constitutional institutions were shaped.
Boundary: Historical memory should support democratic participation, not justify hatred or exclusion.
03 · Sovereignty and unity

Protect India's sovereignty, unity, and integrity

Citizens share a responsibility to protect the constitutional order and the country's democratic unity.

In practice

  • Reject violence and unlawful attempts to destroy constitutional government.
  • Engage disagreements through lawful democratic processes.
Boundary: Peaceful dissent, criticism of government, and legitimate political advocacy remain protected.
04 · Public service

Defend the country and render national service

When called upon, citizens should contribute to the defence of the country and to national service.

In practice

  • Support lawful public safety and emergency efforts.
  • Respond to statutory duties created for national service.
Boundary: Any compulsory service must be created by valid law and applied without discrimination.
05 · Social harmony

Promote harmony and common brotherhood

Rise above religious, linguistic, regional, and sectional differences and reject practices derogatory to the dignity of women.

In practice

  • Challenge discrimination and violence in daily life.
  • Support equal dignity for women and every community.
Boundary: Harmony cannot be used to demand silence from marginalised people or erase legitimate differences.
06–11 · Environment, learning, and care

Protect nature, develop a scientific temper, and strive for excellence

Citizens must value the environment, develop humanism and inquiry, safeguard public property, reject violence, pursue excellence, and provide education opportunities to children aged six to fourteen.

The final six duties

  • Protect forests, lakes, rivers, wildlife, and show compassion for living creatures.
  • Develop scientific temper, humanism, and a spirit of inquiry and reform.
  • Safeguard public property and abjure violence.
  • Strive for excellence in individual and collective activity.
  • Parents or guardians must provide education opportunities to children aged 6–14.
Shared responsibility: These duties guide citizens and lawmakers. Environmental, education, and public-property laws give some of them practical force.

Quick reference

Rights and duties belong together.

Fundamental Duties are found in Article 51A. They are generally non-justiciable on their own, but courts may use them to interpret laws and constitutional values.

Remember

A duty is not a licence to limit a right.

Any restriction on speech, movement, privacy, or equality must still satisfy the Constitution and be supported by valid law.
