Part III · Articles 12–35

Fundamental
rights.

The Constitution gives every person in India a set of protections that guard equality, liberty, dignity, and participation in public life. This guide explains what each right covers and where the law places reasonable limits.

Before you begin

Rights are powerful. They are not unlimited.

Fundamental Rights protect people primarily against arbitrary State action. Some protections apply to everyone, while others are specifically guaranteed to citizens. Restrictions must have a legal basis, serve a legitimate purpose, and meet constitutional standards of fairness.

The constitutional catalogue

Six freedoms, one promise

Articles 14–32
01
Articles 14–18

Right to equality

=

The law must see people as equals and cannot treat similarly placed people differently without a valid reason.

What it includes

  • Equality before law and equal protection of laws.
  • No discrimination by the State on religion, race, caste, sex, or place of birth.
  • Equal opportunity in public employment.
  • Abolition of untouchability and titles.

Limitations & exceptions

Equality permits reasonable classification where there is an intelligible difference and a rational connection to the law's purpose. Protective measures for disadvantaged groups, reservations, and special provisions for women and children can be constitutional forms of equal treatment.

02
Articles 19–22

Right to freedom

↗

Citizens have core civil freedoms, and every person has protection against arbitrary criminal process and loss of liberty.

What it includes

  • Speech and expression, peaceful assembly, association, movement, residence, and profession for citizens.
  • Protection against conviction under retrospective criminal laws and double jeopardy.
  • Life and personal liberty, privacy, dignity, and legal procedure.
  • Rights on arrest, including information and access to a lawyer.

Limitations & exceptions

Article 19 allows reasonable restrictions for sovereignty and integrity, security, public order, decency, morality, contempt of court, defamation, and incitement. Liberty may be restricted by a valid, fair procedure; preventive detention has additional constitutional safeguards.

03
Articles 23–24

Right against exploitation

⊘

No one may be trafficked, forced to work, or pushed into hazardous labour as a child.

What it includes

  • Prohibition of human trafficking and begar or forced labour.
  • Protection from hazardous employment for children below fourteen.
  • Protection applies against private persons as well as the State.
  • Rescue and rehabilitation laws can support these guarantees.

Limitations & exceptions

Compulsory service for a public purpose is allowed if it is not discriminatory. The Constitution does not treat lawful public service or work performed under fair legal conditions as forced labour.

04
Articles 25–28

Freedom of religion

✦

Every person may hold, practise, and share a faith, while India remains committed to secular government and equal citizenship.

What it includes

  • Freedom of conscience and the right to profess, practise, and propagate religion.
  • Religious denominations may manage their own religious affairs.
  • No person can be forced to pay a tax specifically for promoting a religion.
  • Protection from compulsory religious instruction in fully State-funded institutions.

Limitations & exceptions

These freedoms are subject to public order, morality, and health. The State may regulate secular activities linked to religion and make laws for social reform. Religious freedom does not protect violence, coercion, or practices that violate other constitutional guarantees.

05
Articles 29–30

Cultural & educational rights

Aa

Communities can preserve their language and culture, and minorities can establish and administer educational institutions.

What it includes

  • Protection of the language, script, and culture of distinct groups.
  • No citizen may be denied admission to State-maintained institutions on listed grounds.
  • Religious and linguistic minorities may establish and administer institutions.
  • State aid cannot be denied solely because an institution is minority-run.

Limitations & exceptions

Minority institutions remain subject to reasonable regulations for academic standards, public order, health, and administration. The right protects educational identity, not immunity from all regulation or accountability.

06
Article 32

Right to constitutional remedies

↯

When a Fundamental Right is violated, a person can approach the Supreme Court for a constitutional remedy.

What it includes

  • Direct access to the Supreme Court to enforce Fundamental Rights.
  • Courts may issue habeas corpus, mandamus, prohibition, quo warranto, and certiorari.
  • High Courts also protect rights under Article 226 with wider jurisdiction.
  • Legal aid and public interest litigation can improve access to justice.

Limitations & exceptions

Courts grant remedies when a legal right and violation are shown; Article 32 is not a general appeal for every grievance. Parliament may modify how rights apply to armed forces and similar services under Article 33, and rights can be restricted during a constitutionally declared Emergency within Article 359's limits.
