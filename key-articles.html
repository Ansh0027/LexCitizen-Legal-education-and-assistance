12 articles
Article 14 · Equality

Equality before law

Every person is entitled to equality before the law and equal protection of the laws within India.

Why it matters

  • Challenges arbitrary or discriminatory State action.
  • Supports fair classification and equal treatment.
Read with: Articles 15, 16, 17, and 18 expand the equality guarantee.
Article 19 · Civil freedoms

Six freedoms for citizens

Citizens have freedoms of speech and expression, peaceful assembly, association, movement, residence, and profession.

Why it matters

  • Protects democratic debate and association.
  • Creates the starting point for personal and economic liberty.
Limit: Each freedom is subject to specific reasonable restrictions listed in Article 19.
Article 21 · Life and liberty

Life and personal liberty

No person may be deprived of life or personal liberty except according to procedure established by law.

Modern meaning

  • Includes dignity, privacy, health, clean environment, and fair procedure.
  • Applies to citizens and non-citizens.
Limit: Any deprivation must follow valid, fair, and non-arbitrary legal procedure.
Article 32 · Remedies

Approach the Supreme Court

People can ask the Supreme Court to enforce their Fundamental Rights through constitutional writs.

Writ tools

  • Habeas corpus, mandamus, prohibition, quo warranto, and certiorari.
  • High Courts have wider power under Article 226.
Limit: Article 32 is for enforcing Fundamental Rights, not every legal or administrative complaint.
Articles 36–51 · Directive Principles

Directions for governance

Directive Principles guide the State toward social, economic, and political justice, welfare, and equal opportunity.

Examples

  • Equal justice and free legal aid, public health, living wages, and environmental protection.
  • They shape legislation and constitutional interpretation.
Limit: These principles are not directly enforceable in court, but they are fundamental to governance.
Articles 52, 74, 76, 124, 148

Constitutional offices

The Constitution distributes authority among the President, Council of Ministers, Attorney-General, Supreme Court, and Comptroller and Auditor-General.

Why it matters

  • Creates checks, responsibilities, and accountability.
  • Separates constitutional roles from ordinary departments.
Read with: Articles 123, 143, 280, 324, and 356 explain other important institutions and powers.

How to read an Article

Start with the words, then find the context.

  • Read the whole Article, including provisos and explanations.
  • Check related Articles and the relevant Schedule.
  • Look for legislation and judgments that apply it.

A helpful distinction

Rights, principles, and powers are different.

Fundamental Rights can be enforced. Directive Principles guide governance. Constitutional powers define what institutions may do.
